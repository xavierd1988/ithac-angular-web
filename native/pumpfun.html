Pump.fun investigation

Most traders lose on Pump.fun. A few win. We want to know who they are, how they do it, and whether anyone could follow them.

Live test, no real money · the robot runs every 5 minutes

Who are the 26% that win?

A Bitquery study of 3,170 winning positions on Pump.fun (gains of $100,000 or more, October 2025 to August 2026) found that 74% of those gains came from transactions a copy trader cannot repeat. That leaves about 26%, made by ordinary buying and selling. Those are the winners we are looking for.

About the study

The trend robot

Every 5 minutes it reads Google Trends live and the Dinelli's Café edition. As soon as a new trend shows up, it looks for Solana tokens carrying its name, old or new, and buys them on paper. Sold at twice the price, at half, or after 24 hours, 3% costs taken off.

The hourly search

  1. Read the day's trendsFrom the Dinelli's Café morning edition (Google, X, TikTok, YouTube) and Google Trends live.
  2. Find the tokens named after themSolana tokens launched in the past 24 hours whose name matches a trend, in a pool holding at least $1,000.
  3. Test them without moneyBought on paper as soon as we see one, sold at twice the price, at half, or after 24 hours. Prices read every 10 minutes, 3% costs taken off. If the pool is emptied, it counts as a total loss.
  4. Note who bought firstA wallet that keeps getting in early on the tokens that double is a lead.

What we already know

  • Following influencers' calls lost money in our tests. Following each day's top 10 of the ITHAC ranking lost $1,019 over 257 calls (October 30 to November 25, $100 per call, costs included).
  • The big winners were moving before anyone called them. The coins that rose the most after a first call had already gained 14% in the 3 days before it, against 0% for the others.
  • On news coins, minutes count. On the token tied to the GTA VI leak, the five biggest winners bought within six minutes of each other, 48 to 54 minutes after the leak, and a human trader who came in two minutes after the first bot still made money (Bitquery, August 2026).
  • Some influencers get in before their own tweet. In a case shown by Bubblemaps in October 2026, five hidden wallets bought about 20 seconds before a recommendation and sold about four minutes after it.
